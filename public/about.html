MTB Skills Assessment

A rubric-based skill assessment tool for NICA MTB coaches — Andrew Shaber, Renee Kline & Tim Curry

Why this exists

Ask a mountain bike coach the skill level of their riders and the answer is almost universally: "They're really fast." That's answering the wrong question.

The consequence is real. A coach picks up an unfamiliar rider, assumes competence, and discovers the gap in the middle of a difficult trail. NICA's instructor training uses a 1–5 scale for individual skills — but without objective measures behind the numbers, the scale is unreliable across coaches and teams. Measuring skills in isolation, especially in a parking lot at the start of the season, isn't a strong predictor of trail readiness.

This rubric is the attempt to fix that. Andrew Shaber, Renee Kline, and Tim Curry co-founded the Boise Foothills MTB team and kept running into the same problems. The rubric grew out of coaching practice, not theory — refined over multiple seasons with real athletes on real trails.

What makes this rubric different

Skill is not binary. The rubric measures the full progression within each skill. Three foundational skills — body position, braking, cornering — across five levels defined by two parallel progressions:

  • Environment and consistency — controlled → sometimes → most of the time → always → bombproof
  • Skill accumulation — each level adds specific teaching points. The rubric describes what breaks, when it breaks, and at what threshold.

Body position is the foundation. A rider can't brake effectively if their weight is wrong. They can't corner if they can't brake. The three skills build on each other in order.

LevelCornering example
1Seated, steering with handlebars, eyes fixed on ground ahead
2Begins to look ahead; outside foot down inconsistently; braking in corners
3Low/look/lean emerging; breaks down on rough or off-camber terrain
4Full technique most of the time; recovers from small errors; manages blue terrain
5Speed on black-plus terrain with roots and rocks; failures rare and self-corrected

Motor learning alignment

The progression reflects the Fitts and Posner stages of motor learning used in NICA coach training:

  • Levels 1–2: Cognitive stage — movements are conscious and inconsistent. The rider is thinking about what to do.
  • Level 3: Associative — movements becoming reliable but degrading under pressure or on unfamiliar terrain.
  • Level 4: Largely autonomous — foundational movements require no conscious attention, freeing the rider for terrain reading and tactics.
  • Level 5: Fully autonomous — applied on the most demanding terrain; errors are self-detected and self-corrected.

A note on the OTB curriculum: NICA's OTB 101/201/301 teaches the positive — what to do. This rubric is the complementary tool. It describes failure modes because error detection and correction requires knowing what breakdown looks like. Different purposes, same progression.

Trail readiness

Confirmed skill levels map to trail difficulty. The app shows which trails a rider is genuinely ready for — not which trails they've survived. That distinction matters when building a practice route or deciding whether a rider is ready to step up.

A rider who has done a black trail once, on a good day, with a patient group, is not "black ready." Confirmed levels require consistency — the rubric defines what consistent looks like at each level. Trail readiness is computed from the lowest confirmed skill across body position, braking, and cornering.

Using the app

Works fully offline — no network needed on trail. No login required. Data stays on your device.

First time setup

  • 1
    Open Settings (bottom tab) and enter your name and team. This tags every observation you log.
  • 2
    Go to Roster and tap + Add to add your athletes. Name and role (athlete or coach) are all you need.

Logging an observation

You don't need to stop or open a form. On the Roster tab, each athlete row shows three level pills — one per skill. Tap the pill for the skill you're watching and select a level 1–5. That's it — observation logged with a timestamp.

For more detail — history, trend graph, full skill breakdown — tap the athlete's name to open their full card.

Confirming a level

Observations are raw data. A confirmed level is your judgment call that a rider has demonstrated consistent skill at that level. Open the athlete's full card, select the skill, and tap Confirm. The trail readiness view updates immediately.

The app surfaces history to support your judgment but never auto-promotes — one good rep does not confirm a level.

Running a practice

  • 1
    Go to the Practice tab and tap Start Practice.
  • 2
    Tap Take Attendance — toggle each rider present. Attending riders sort to the top of the Roster for the rest of practice.
  • 3
    Log observations from the Roster tab as normal during practice.
  • 4
    When you're done, go back to Practice and tap End Practice. A quick sheet lets you rate how practice went (😞–😊), add a reflection note, and log any incidents or safety concerns. All optional — tap Skip to close immediately.

Sharing an athlete card

Every athlete card has a QR code visible on their full card. A coach on another device opens the app, taps the scan icon on the Roster tab, and scans the QR — the athlete's profile and skill data import instantly. No account, no network required. This is the fastest way to hand off a rider when they change pods or join a clinic group.

Exporting data

  • Full roster export — Settings tab → Export JSON. All athletes, observations, confirmed levels, and practice records in a single file. Use this to back up your data or move it to another device.
  • Practice report — on an ended practice card in the Practice tab, tap the export button. Downloads attendance, reflection, and incident notes for that practice.

FAQ

Does this replace OTB coach training?
No. OTB training teaches coaching skills and safety procedures. This rubric compliments OTB classes; it is an assessment tool — it provides a guage of where a rider is in their progression in a way that can be communicated to other coaches.
How long does it take to assess a rider?
Just as skills are taught throughout a season, skills should be continually assessed. All it takes is when following on trail, or for skill sessioning, record the observation. Over time you'll see the trend in rider's skill and can confirm changed level.
Is there a version for Athletes to use?
An early prototype is in the works. Feedback very appreciated: Athlete App
Is there a version for Coaches to use?
An early prototype is in the works. Feedback very appreciated: Coach App
Is there a version for Head Coach to use?
An early prototype is in the works. Feedback very appreciated: Head Coach App
Can the app be used for a full league?
The data model is designed for multi-team use, but right now each coach runs their own local instance. League-wide sync and reporting is planned for Phase 4. Interested in early access? Get in touch.
Does it work without internet?
Yes, fully. Once you've loaded the app, it runs entirely from your browser cache. Open it before you leave the trailhead. All data is stored locally on your device.
Where is the data stored?
Everything stays on your device in browser localStorage. Nothing is sent to a server. Export JSON from Settings to back up your data or move it to another device.
What if a rider changes groups?
You can share individual athlete cards by QR code. On the full athlete card, the 3 dot menu has a share button. Scan the QR code in the receiving coaches app. Full multi-coach sync is on the roadmap for Phase 3.
Is there an athlete or head coach view?
There's an early prototype — healthprof.github.io/mtb_skill_concept — with athlete, coach, head coach, and admin views. Feedback welcome.
