MTB Skills Assessment

A coach-facing skill assessment tool for NICA mountain bike coaches.

What this is

This tool grew out of a collaboration between Andrew Shaber and Tim Curry, coaches in the Idaho NICA league. We wanted a structured, offline-first way to assess rider skills on trail — no paper, no cell service needed.

The rubric is based on Tim's field assessment cards, adapted for easy tap-to-score use on a phone during a ride. Every observation is timestamped, every confirmed level is explicit — and trail readiness is computed automatically from the rubric minimums.

It's free, open source, and works entirely in your browser — no account, no backend, no subscription.

How assessment works

  • 1
    Observe — watch a rider and tap the level that matches what you see. The app logs the observation with a timestamp.
  • 2
    Confirm — once behavior is consistent, set their confirmed level. Confirmed levels are coach judgment, not auto-promoted from observations.
  • 3
    Trail ready — trail readiness is computed from confirmed levels against the rubric minimums for Green, Blue, Black, and Double Black terrain.

Three skills are assessed: Body Position, Braking, and Cornering. Each is scored 1–5 on a rubric developed from real coaching observations.

FAQ

Does it work without internet?
Yes, fully. Once you've loaded the app, it runs entirely in your browser from cached files. Open it before you leave the trailhead. All data is stored in localStorage on your device.
Where is the data stored?
Everything stays on your device in browser localStorage. Nothing is sent to a server. Export JSON from Settings to back up your data or move it to another device.
Can multiple coaches share data?
Right now, data lives on each device separately. You can share individual athlete cards by QR code (athlete card → share). Full multi-coach sync is on the roadmap for Phase 3.
What does each skill level mean?
Open the Field Guide tab in the app for the full rubric — all 5 levels for Body Position, Braking, and Cornering with dimensions, failure modes, and video links.
Is this specific to Idaho NICA?
The rubric came from Idaho, but the tool works for any NICA league or MTB coaching program. The trail minimums follow standard NICA trail difficulty conventions.
